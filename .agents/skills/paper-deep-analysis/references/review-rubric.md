# Human review rubric

Rate each dimension as `pass`, `needs_revision`, or `not_assessable`; add an
evidence-based note. Do not average these into a fake accuracy score.

- Identity: title, authors, identifiers, version, and dates match the source.
- Fidelity: contribution and method are represented without inflated novelty.
- Narrative clarity: the report reconstructs the paper's context, prior gap,
  importance, solution, and findings before assuming specialist familiarity.
- Executive-summary fidelity: the prominent core paragraph identifies what the
  paper studies, how it studies it, and what it finds; analyst evaluation and
  evidence limits remain visibly separate.
- Executive-summary accessibility: a curious non-specialist can understand the
  two-sentence hook in one read; it is concise, formula-free, and contains no
  unexplained abbreviation or paper-native jargon.
- Evidence: major statements point to supporting pages/sections.
- Critical depth: baseline fairness, ablations, limitations, and alternatives are
  assessed rather than merely listed.
- Transfer value: derived lessons are conditional, specific, and actionable.
- Profile value: selected domain overlay answers real user needs.
- Concept clarity: key terms have a formal definition, plain-language
  explanation, relevance, and traceable source.
- Transparency: full-text/abstract-only basis, model, uncertainty, and extraction
  issues are visible.
- Dataset utility: records are atomic, attributed, deduplicated, and traceable.
- HTML usability: hierarchy, current-section navigation, typography, spacing,
  mobile, reduced-motion behavior, and print work; metric values never collide,
  and paired labels and prose share a clean top edge.
- Visual honesty: key metrics remain paper-reported and anchored; no unexplained
  evidence meter, claim-strength distribution, or pseudo-accuracy graphic is
  present.
- Publication safety: no source PDF, full text, local paths, credentials, or
  unsupported claims are present.
